# The "cost cap" is a stop-width floor, and what that says about the engine (2026-09-07)

Round 2 established that forex's apparent profit was a feed artifact and that
on the witness's contained years the class loses money capped or uncapped.
This note answers what follows, and it is the question the desk's unparking
turns on: **on data we trust, does this engine earn money in any
configuration?** It also corrects, in public, the framing this note carried
in its first draft.

**Provenance, stated once and honestly.** Figures marked *(tracked)* come from
a pass of this session's own and are reproducible from the artifacts beside

`cap-x-floor`, `cap-sweep-null`, `crypto-cell`, `cost-is-constant`,

## 1. The answer

**No configuration earns money on clean data.** Swept over eight classes and
seven cap values plus no cap on contained rows *(tracked)*, the program's own
acceptance gate (D4) accepts **no class cell at any cap**; its earns-money
term is positive in exactly three cells, all thin — futures at 0.05 and 0.075
and metals at 0.05. A wider round sweep over nine cap values reports the same
three of seventy-two *(round)*. Every cell that looked like an exception
failed a check: futures' earners reverse on the held-out markets, and crypto's
one survivor is 72 % carried by 2021.

## 2. What the cap actually is

The rule is `estimatedRoundTripCost / riskDistance ≤ cap`. In forex it carries
no cost information at all. Measured over all 229,922 contained forex rows:
`estimatedRoundTripCost` is **1.17 basis points of price on 99.624 %** of them
(median, mode and minimum all 0.000117). So the rule is algebraically a
**minimum relative stop width** — `riskDistance ≥ price × 0.000117 / cap`,
which is 7.8 bps of price at the shipped 0.15 and 11.7 bps at 0.10 — and the
two rules classify **zero rows differently at any of the six swept caps**.

This is a forex property: the modal cost share is 99.6 % of rows in forex and
0.0–35.7 % in every other class, because forex's cost model is a flat spread
in basis points.

So the first draft of this note was wrong where it mattered most. The lever is
not "declining expensive trades". It is **requiring a wider stop**. Its own
placebo table listed "lowest risk distance" as a control; risk distance is not
a control for this rule, it is the rule.

**The other stop axis says the tails are both bad, not that wider is better.**
The stop-cap arm, swept independently on contained rows, found tightening
`maxStopAtrMultiplier` destructive — pooled −3,114.2 R at 1.5 on fit and
−2,994.5 R on select *(round)*. But round 2 found the opposite extreme is bad
too: `maxStopAtrMultiplier=8`, act 3's "only swept stop cell accepted
anywhere", reads **−196.4 R on 2018–2020 select and is negative in every clean
year** (`feed-window-round2-2026-09-07.md`). Forex ships 4, between them. So
the honest shape of this axis on clean data is **non-monotonic — both tails
lose** — and the corroboration it offers the stop-width floor is weaker than
"two knobs, one finding". What the two agree on is only that the shipped
geometry is not at a measured optimum, and that the direction from 0.15 is
toward a wider minimum, not that wider is better without limit.

Every tightening from 0.15 gains on both folds. **The direction is not
monotone on both folds** — an error in the first draft. Select improves at
every step; fit peaks at 0.10 and falls away. The two folds disagree on where
to stop, which is exactly what a swept optimum looks like, and it is why the
evidence identifies a direction and not the number 0.10.

The 0.15 → 0.10 move is +651.1 R on fit and +679.4 R on select *(tracked)*.
Against a null that keeps the same *number* of rows drawn at random from the
stream the shipped cap already keeps, **+329.9 R of the select figure is the
arithmetic of trading 34 % less of a losing stream**, leaving +349.4 R of
genuine discrimination (z +5.33); on fit the null is −72.0 R, so the
discrimination is +723.1 R (z +7.84) *(tracked,
`tighten-decompose-2026-09-07.txt`)*. The same null takes +124.3 R out of the
shipped cap's own +353.7 R headline, so this is a property of every declining
gate on a losing stream and must be stated wherever such a gate is priced.

Three attacks found nothing *(round, not re-measured here)*: the held-out
exclusion is not the driver (restoring all 28 markets makes the delta larger,

grain down to calendar-year blocks (t 5.98 fit, 7.40 select at date-block
level); and the month grain removes only 1.58 % of contained rows and leaves
the deltas standing.

## 4. Why it still ships nothing

- **D4 has no fit-fold expectancy term.** Its earns-money test is
  `rExpectancyLower95(variant.select) > 0` (`grid-totalr.ts:918`) and nothing
  else; the fit fold enters only as `fitTotalDelta > 0`. The first draft's
  "the fit fold clears D4's own-expectancy term" named a term that does not
  exist. D4's money test fails at 0.10, and at every cap, in every class.
- **Volume collapses before profit arrives.** At cap 0.05 metals keeps 11 of
  2,507 select fills *(tracked)*; twelve of twenty-four crypto markets, four
  of five agriculture markets and all five indices markets keep zero or two,
  and applying the gate's own thin floor at the market grain **zero of
  seventy-four markets qualify at 0.05 in any class** *(round)*.
- **Both clean folds end in 2020.** Forex's escaping years begin in 2021, so
  every readable post-2020 forex row is escaping — the contained fit fold has
  no escaping rows at all. On the escaping select rows the same tightening
  **costs −549.9 R**, taking +2,877.4 R to +2,327.6 R *(tracked)*. The clean
  evidence is a 2009–2020 statement.
- **Per market**, `cap-per-market-2026-09-07.txt` measures no-cap → 0.10, not
  0.15 → 0.10: on that comparison the move is negative on **5 of 22** markets
  on fit (AUDJPY, CADJPY, CHFJPY, EURCAD, EURNZD) and 1 of 22 on select
  (GBPNZD), with 71 % of the fit gain carried by five markets *(tracked)*. The
  same per-market split for the 0.15 → 0.10 step has not been measured here.

## 5. What survives, and what it points at

What survives is a direction, measured on clean data and cross-validated by a
second axis: **forex's stops are too tight, and widening them earns money on
both clean folds.** What does not survive is a value, a class-grain
acceptance, or a profit.

Every other axis measured across five arms — runner protection, stop source,
review window, class default, the per-symbol derived layer — moves clean-years
money **down**. The per-symbol layer earns its keep (+16,141 R on fit against
the class default) and forcing breakeven protection costs −7,572 R across the
two folds, which is the measurement behind amendment 42. *(Those two figures
are a workflow agent's, reproduced by its verifier, not by a pass of mine.)*

At cap 0.10 forex's clean select expectancy is −0.0087 R per fill. The entry
signal's gross edge is roughly its costs, short by about one point of risk.
**No admission rule and no stop-width floor creates gross edge**; both can
only re-shape what is there. That is what amendment 39 says about levers of
this kind, and it is why the next gain has to come from analysis rather than
from configuration.
