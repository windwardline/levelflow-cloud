## Decision memo — TipRanks, the Ultimate entitlements, and the sequence

**Prepared 2026-09-12. Every number below that is labelled MEASURED I ran myself today against the sealed-fold doors; 2,141,527 confirm rows were byte-tested and skipped before any JSON parse, and no confirm outcome was read.**

---

### 1. TipRanks: cancel it

The add-on is already a paid line on top of Ultimate, so the action is cancellation, not declination.

**The decisive fact is that there is nothing to point it at.** MEASURED, one pass over `docs/research/r3/capture-all-classfolds.jsonl`: across both tuning folds the corpus holds 439,939 filled baseline accepted rows (fit 272,503, select 167,436) over 91 markets. The seven US-equity-index-linked instruments — SP, NSDQ, DOW, ESUSD, NQUSD, RTYUSD, YMUSD — account for **5,345 of them, 1.215%**. The largest single market-fold cell in that set is ESUSD's fit fold at 732 fills. MEASURED on the same rows, the per-fill standard deviation of realized R is **0.6737**, so a one-sided 95% lower bound clears zero at +0.03 R/fill only above ~1,366 fills, and at +0.02 R/fill only above ~3,074. No equity-linked market reaches either floor on either fold. Amendment 33 forbids the pooling that would fake it, and pooling all seven in-pool-and-held-out still lands under 3,000 with three of the seven on the wrong side of the holdout wall. A TipRanks rule could not earn a verdict on this corpus even if the signal were real.

**Two corrections to the brief I was given, both recorded because a correction that repeats the defect is worse than the original.** The brief said 2,730 filled rows of 346,226, and placed NSDQ, RTYUSD and YMUSD in the holdout with SP, ESUSD and NQUSD in-pool. MEASURED, the holdout is the other way round: **SP and NQUSD are held out; NSDQ, DOW, ESUSD, RTYUSD and YMUSD are in-pool** (18 holdout symbols in total). DOW carries 43 select fills and **zero fit-fold rows**, so it cannot be fit and then confirmed at all. My share is 1.215%, not 0.79%. The correction makes the surface *larger* and the verdict is unchanged, which is the point of recording it.

**Second reason: it cannot be validated against the folds that decide things here.** The brief's premise that a ~3-year history misses everything is wrong, and the truth is more specific. MEASURED from the corpus manifest's `foldsByClass`: forex fit runs 2009-09-25 → 2018-03-06 and select 2018-03-11 → 2022-05-29 — unreachable. But indices fit runs 2020-02-24 → 2023-05-22 and futures fit+select runs 2023-09-24 → 2025-11-27, which a 3-year history does reach. That is not a rescue. It means any TipRanks test would run entirely on post-2023 data — one macro regime, on the classes with the fewest fills, in a project that has already been burned by exactly that failure (the verified 2021 forex regime break). And every one of those markets' confirm folds is spent: the ledger `docs/research/confirm-reads/confirm-log-f3b72ce8….jsonl` is a single line, read 2026-09-03T10:09:18Z, carrying **97 confirmSpans** — ESUSD and NQUSD burned from 2025-12-02, SP from 2025-01-09, forex from 2022-06-03, all to 2026-08-26. There is no unread door left to confirm such a rule behind.

**Third: Ultimate already carries the layer anyone here would use.** Price target summary and consensus, ratings snapshot, historical ratings, stock grades and historical grades are included at no extra cost. The genuine delta is per-analyst identity and TipRanks' own star metrics — vendor-computed backward-looking performance scores, unauditable from this repo, which is precisely the class of input the shadow-test rule exists to refuse.

The price is unpublished and the verdict does not depend on it. At zero it still fails: integration costs a change set, and there is no instrument to act on.

**The reopener requires all four, not any one:** single-name equities or equity CFDs on the roster and registered in the corpus manifest; those markets carrying at least the then-current power floor per market per fold with sigma recomputed from that day's corpus; `grid-totalr` able to return a money verdict on a selective admission rule; and the engine earning positive net R on an existing class on clean contained data. Condition four is the question the desk is parked on. Condition one is E8's Stocks tab, which has never been captured — owner work, not repo work.

### 2. The entitlements — what actually changes

**Two entitlements bear, and one of them is smaller than it looks.**

*Bulk and batch delivery* was deferred on 2026-07-02 because the symbol list was "small enough for controlled per-symbol requests", and the 2026-08-13 and 2026-08-27 exhaustions falsified that premise. It should stay deferred anyway, for a better reason. MEASURED on `.calibration-cache` (96 symbols per timeframe): **5-minute 5.70 GB, 15-minute 1.93 GB, daily 0.04 GB.** Daily bars are 0.5% of the cached bar volume. The exhaustions were bought by intraday history, and the entitlement register lists no bulk or batch form of intraday charts — the bulk family is EOD and statements. Batch quotes and batch forex quotes are real and cheap to adopt, but `/quote` is one small response per symbol per scan; that is not where the allowance went. Recommendation: adopt batch quotes opportunistically when the live path is next touched, and stop citing bulk delivery as a lever against the allowance.

*Market hours and holidays by exchange* — the endpoint is keyed by exchange (`holidays-by-exchange?exchange=NASDAQ`, [FMP docs](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/developer/docs/stable/holidays-by-exchange)). Only 36 of 97 markets have an exchange at all; forex and crypto, 61 of the roster and the entire hour finding, have none. Both refuters killed the calendar program on measurement: on real closure dates the corpus already carries zero decisions, because the provider prints no bars and `replay.ts:375-377` resolves bar-by-bar rather than by clock. The absence of data is already the closure model and it already works. **Do not buy session data to fix a defect that does not exist.**

*Full quote per class, technical indicators, economic indicators, index constituents, deeper COT* — no change. Technical indicators would spend bytes to buy arithmetic the engine already computes. Economic indicators carry a look-ahead trap that is a program in itself, not an entitlement.

**Everything else is equity data with no instrument here**: company reference, statements, DCF, SEC filings, transcripts, 13F, congressional disclosures, insider trades, ETF and fund holdings, ESG, IPOs, dividends and splits, and the analyst suite. Not defects — the rest of a general-purpose API.

The honest summary: **Ultimate unlocks almost nothing this project can act on.** The binding constraint is gross edge on forex, and no endpoint on the entitlement list sells that.

### 7. What the refuters killed, and why

**Cost model by hour of day — dead, both sides.** The instrument cannot reach the folds: forex fit ends 2018-03-06 and select 2022-05-29, and no entitlement on Ultimate sells historical bid/ask, so any census is forward-looking and would re-price evidence that predates it. And the ceiling is too small to matter: the gross-minus-net gap is 0.0183 R/fill in span against 0.0236 out, spread is 52% of it, so driving the in-span spread to zero gains at most +0.0096 R against a measured in/out differential of 0.0461 R. Re-pricing a ledger is not money.

**Exchange holiday and session calendar — dead.** The headline was falsified by the corpus that was supposed to prove it. On every date the venue is actually shut the corpus already carries zero decisions. On CME full-closure dates, 66 of 81 exchange-traded decision rows sit 18:00–23:59 ET — the Globex reopen for the next trade date, when the venue was open. Holiday-decided rows are 796 of 44,344 exchange-traded fills at −87.0 R, a difference from other days of −0.0317 R/fill with a 95% interval spanning zero. Meanwhile 20.3% of exchange-traded decisions have review windows crossing the class's own nightly maintenance break, which `src/lib/marketHours.ts:51-55` already models and `replay.ts:827-844` already ignores — 203× the exposure, no calendar, no provider, no new data. That is the real defect in this subject and it is free.

**Economic-surprise forward banking — dead.** Its only argument was irreversibility, and the premise is false. ALFRED serves true point-in-time vintages for free ([FRED docs](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_observations.html)), and a consensus forecast published before a release is a fixed historical fact that cannot be revised. With no option decay, a program whose entire value is optionality has none.

**The unread-column census split the refuters, and its headline is dead.** Fifteen never-graded decision-time fields do carry a numeric value on every tuning-fold row of every market — that part survives. But the claim that a lever's sign is backwards is a category error: `macroRates.ts:296-300` makes `magnitude` a multiplier on a signed direction call, the offered evidence pools aligned and against rows, and the sign test run directly says the contrast flips between folds with every difference inside its own interval. The money path is closed regardless — 72 of 97 markets carry `confidenceThreshold = 0`, including all 28 forex, and the macro term decided the admission of 40 rows out of 638,178, worth −15.27 R. The one strong candidate the census surfaced, `volatilityPercentile`, reverses under a `costShare` control and holds reversed on gross R, where no modelled cost can be blamed. If the census runs, it runs as fifteen fields with the macro lever struck out and `cotPercentile` dropped to the 16 markets with a complete column — 45 of 91 markets have no COT value anywhere, and the reader throws on the first null.
